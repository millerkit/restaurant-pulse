3. OPTIONS FOR THE MISSED DEPRECIATION

A. Amend 2025 (Form 1040-X). 2025 is open.
   Candidates: the old dishwasher disposal (about $2,800 more deduction), the new dishwasher
   ($449, or up to $3,144 with Section 179), and the ice machine ($1,000) if not deducted elsewhere.
   Roughly $4,300 to $6,900 of added deductions. At a combined 25-40% rate (federal, Massachusetts and
   self-employment tax, if the Schedule C had profit), roughly $1,100 to $2,800 of tax.

B. Years 2022-2024.
   Depreciation missed for two or more consecutive years is normally treated as an impermissible
   accounting method, corrected with Form 3115 (automatic method change, with a Section 481(a)
   adjustment), not by amended returns. The adjustment is taken in the year of change.
   Timing: a 2022 refund claim is probably closed (3 years from filing, which ended April-October 2026).
   2023 and 2024 are still open for 1040-X.
   ACCOUNTANT: can the change still be made for 2025, or should it go on the 2026 return? Do assets
   sold in 2026 qualify for the method change?

C. Do nothing on the missed items.
   Basis is reduced by depreciation "allowed or allowable", so unclaimed depreciation does not help us:
   it only increases the taxable gain when the assets are sold.

WORTH KNOWING: for assets that are being sold in 2026, claiming missed depreciation mostly shifts income
between years. Each dollar of added deduction becomes a dollar of extra depreciation recapture (ordinary
income) on the sale. The lasting savings come from rate differences, self-employment tax (recapture on
Form 4797 is not subject to it), the dishwasher loss, and any asset the buyer does not take. This may
decide how much effort the amendments are worth.
